How I build AI applications with Python

Most teams fail with AI not because the model is weak, but because the pipeline around it is fragile. In my projects I keep three layers deliberately simple: a clean FastAPI or Django service, a thin LLM client behind an interface, and explicit error handling for timeouts and malformed JSON. That separation means I can swap a model provider without touching business logic.

Read the full article

Prompt engineering gets most of the attention, but evaluation is what makes an AI feature production-ready. I write a small test set of realistic inputs for every tool I ship, and I run them on every change. If a resume-parsing tool returns a wrong skills list, I want to catch it before a client does.

For deployment, a containerised service behind an API gateway keeps costs low and response times predictable. Caching is your best friend here — most requests can be answered with a cache hit instead of a fresh model call.

How ATS systems evaluate resumes

Applicant tracking systems do not "reject" resumes — they filter them. They parse your text, extract keywords, and score you against the job description. The candidates who get interviews are usually the ones who made parsing effortless and matched the language of the role.

Read the full article

Use standard section headings like Work Experience and Skills. Avoid tables, images, and text boxes — they break parsing. Include a summary that mirrors the job's own terms, and quantify achievements with numbers wherever you can.

Finally, tailor one resume per application. The single most effective change I make for clients is aligning their opening summary to the exact title and top keywords of the target role.

Best Python projects for businesses

The most valuable Python work for a business is rarely glamorous. It is the invoice generator, the reporting dashboard, the CRM sync, or the scraper that removes a weekly hour of manual copying. Automation pays for itself in weeks, not years.

Read the full article

I look for one rule when proposing projects: the script should eliminate a task someone does repeatedly and reliably. A report that a manager reads every Monday morning is worth more than a clever model nobody uses.

Small, boring automation builds trust. Once a business trusts your code, the same relationship extends to bigger bets — internal tools, integrations, and finally AI features that change how they operate.
